# Mayor Wiki

## What Does The Mayor of Toronto Do?

The mayor is the head of the City Council and the city's chief executive officer.

The mayor is elected by voters across the entire city, while councillors are elected to represent individual wards.

The mayor's role is to provide leadership to Council and to represent Toronto. The mayor also has specific responsibilities and powers under the [City of Toronto Act, 2006](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/accountability-operations-customer-service/city-administration/city-managers-office/intergovernmental-affairs/city-of-toronto-act/) and other provincial legislation.

The general responsibilities of the mayor are:

- Lead and coordinate the work of the City Council
- Represent Toronto across Canada and internationally
- Work with provincial and federal governments
- Organize the City Council's committee system and appoint committee chairs
- Propose the city's annual budget

The mayor has additional [Strong Mayor](#what-are-the-strong-mayor-powers) powers that can influence certain City Council decisions, which are discussed further below.

## How Does The Mayor Lead City Council?

The mayor has an important role in organizing City Council's work. The mayor can establish or dissolve certain committees, assign their responsibilities, and appoint their chairs and vice-chairs.

Committees allow smaller groups of councillors to focus on specific areas of city government, such as housing, planning, infrastructure, or economic development. For more information on the City’s Committees, check out our [Committees Wiki](https://civicdashboard.ca/wiki/committeesWiki.html).

The mayor chairs the Executive Committee, which considers issues affecting the city's overall direction, including major strategic, financial, and governance matters.

The Executive Committee makes recommendations to the City Council, which makes the final decision on matters that are within its authority.

## How Does The Mayor Represent Toronto?

The mayor is Toronto's main political representative to other governments, including the Government of Ontario and the Government of Canada. The mayor advocates for Toronto's interests, negotiates with other governments, and works to secure funding and agreements for the city. This is an important part of the mayor's role because many issues affecting Toronto involve multiple levels of government.

For example, transit is shared between the City and the Province. The [TTC](https://www.ttc.ca/) is a City agency, while [Metrolinx](https://www.metrolinx.com/) and [GO Transit](https://www.gotransit.com/) are provincial responsibilities. Major projects can therefore require the mayor to work with the Premier, provincial ministers and Metrolinx to advocate for Toronto's priorities and coordinate with the City.

The mayor's role is therefore not just to represent Toronto within City Hall, but also to represent Toronto in dealings with the provincial and federal governments.

## How Does the Mayor Work With the Toronto Public Service?

The Toronto Public Service is the city's professional workforce. It operates city programs and services and implements City Council decisions.

The mayor can appoint certain senior leaders of the Toronto Public Service, including the City Manager, who is responsible for the day-to-day administration of the City.

The mayor also has powers to appoint or dismiss certain other senior city officials. These powers are part of the city's [Strong Mayor](#what-are-the-strong-mayor-powers) powers and are subject to the rules set out in provincial law.

## What Are the Strong Mayor Powers?

In 2022, the Province of Ontario gave Toronto's mayor additional powers through the [Strong Mayors, Building Homes Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/s22018).

These powers give the mayor greater authority over certain areas of city government, including:

- Council committees
- The city's organizational structure
- The city budget
- Certain council meetings and decisions
- Matters related to provincial priorities

The mayor must exercise these special powers in writing, and mayoral decisions are made publicly available.

### Provincial Priorities

The mayor can bring certain matters to the City Council if they believe they could advance a provincial priority.

The mayor can also veto certain bylaws if they believe they could interfere with a provincial priority.

Ontario's current [provincial priorities](https://www.ontario.ca/document/ontario-municipal-councillors-guide/10-strong-mayor-powers-and-duties) for this purpose are:

- Building 1.5 million new homes in Ontario by December 31, 2031
- Building and maintaining infrastructure needed to support housing, including transit, roads, utilities, and servicing

The mayor’s veto applies only to certain types of bylaws, particularly those involving municipal powers such as planning and development. It does not give the mayor a general veto over City Council decisions.

A vetoed bylaw does not take effect, but City Council can override the mayor with a two-thirds vote.

### The City Budget

The mayor has the formal responsibility to propose Toronto's annual budget, but the mayor does not develop it alone. The budget is developed and reviewed collaboratively by:

- City financial staff
- The City Manager
- The Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer
- City divisions and agencies
- The Budget Committee

Once the mayor presents the proposed budget, the City Council can amend it subject to the mayor’s veto powers. This gives the mayor significant influence over the budget, but Council still has the final say if it can assemble a two-thirds majority.
